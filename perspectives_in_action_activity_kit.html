Page 2 · Perspective Cards

Environmental value systems at a glance

Use the cards below to guide your argument. Each perspective values the environment differently, so each may support different behaviours and decisions.

Ecocentric

Protect nature first

Nature has intrinsic value. Protect ecosystems even if humans must reduce development.

Anthropocentric

Support people and wellbeing

Nature is important mainly because it supports human life and wellbeing.

Technocentric

Innovate and engineer solutions

Technology and innovation can solve environmental problems.

Mixed Perspective

Balance needs and limits

A balance between environmental protection, human needs, and practical development.

Page 4 · Student Simulation Task

Your Task

Move from reading to action. The process below keeps the activity discussion-based, structured, and fast enough for one lesson segment.

1

Read your perspective card.

Notice what matters most in that viewpoint before you decide anything.

2

Choose or receive one scenario.

Focus on one environmental decision so your group can respond clearly.

3

Discuss in pairs or groups what your perspective would do.

Agree on the action your perspective supports and the reason behind it.

4

Defend your action in a mini debate.

Use evidence, values, and consequences to justify your response.

5

Compare how different perspectives changed behaviour.

Look for patterns in decisions, trade-offs, and priorities.
